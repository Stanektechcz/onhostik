**Outcome.** A hosting that is removed stops pointing its DNS at the node. Until now the customer's domain kept
resolving to a node that no longer served their site, and their mail kept being delivered to a node that no longer
accepts it — with SPF and DKIM still authorising it.

**Files.**

- `domains/Dns/ServiceDnsCleanup.php` (new) — removes what this service published: `web:<service>`,
  `service:<service>` and `mail:<domain>` for every mail domain (from the bindings **and** the `mail_domains` rows,
  because the mail-domain step deletes the binding before this one runs), in every zone of the organization that
  covers one of the service's names, plus the hosting hostname in a platform zone. Records the customer made
  themselves are `managed_by = customer` and are never touched.
- `domains/Provisioning/Workflows/ServiceActionWorkflow.php` — `platformDnsStep` delegates to it and publishes
  `service.purge.leftover` (kind `dns`) for a zone that would not take the removal.
- `tests/Feature/Dns/ServiceDnsCleanupTest.php` (new), events catalog, audit row 100, `CURRENT_STATE.md`.

**Contracts.** `service.purge.leftover` gains the kind `dns`. Nothing else.

**Verification.**

- VERIFIED — failing-first, through the whole purge saga: the zone still held `@ A`, `www A`, `@ MX` and the SPF
  `TXT` after the service was gone, and nothing was reported.
- VERIFIED — after the fix only the customer's own `office A` row is left.
- VERIFIED — with PowerDNS refusing the change the service still ends and `service.purge.leftover` is published.
- VERIFIED — the "the reconciler retries" claim in the old comment was read to the end and is false:
  `DnsService::commit()` calls the provider first and returns without touching the database on a failure, so the
  platform and the provider agree and `checkDrift()` finds nothing.
- VERIFIED — gates: Pint clean, Larastan 0 errors, Pest **923 tests / 14 896 assertions** green.
- NOT TESTED — nothing ran against a live PowerDNS or ISPConfig.

**Concerns.**

1. **A failed `DnsChange` batch is still nobody's work.** `commit()` marks the batch `failed` and nothing ever
   picks it up — not only here. A nightly retry of *system* changes would close it, but it writes into live
   customer zones, so it needs a decision first (the standing rule is that existing services and customers are not
   touched).
2. **Zones that already carry the records of services that ended earlier.** Not swept, deliberately. A staging
   query would find them: system records whose `comment` names a service that is TERMINATED or gone.
