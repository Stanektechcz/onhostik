**Outcome.** `PlanPromises`'s guard no longer trusts the price list as proof a number is applied. A new
`domains/Services/Metering/MetricRegistry.php` records, per entitlement/limit key, what actually measures or
enforces it today (status `measured` / `enforced_only` / `gap`, with sources and reasons). `PlanPromises` checks
every numeric (and, where a genuine gap surfaced, boolean) key against that registry, `FAIR_USE`, or a new
`KNOWN_GAPS` ratchet constant (may only shrink); non-numeric capability flags otherwise keep the original text-scan
rule. `onhost:doctor` shows the known-gap list as a standing WARN and any *new*, untracked gap as FAIL. No prices,
seeder, services or adapters changed — only the guard's honesty.

**Concerns — please confirm.** The task brief said "keep non-numeric capability flags on the current rule," meaning
`KNOWN_GAPS` was described for numeric promises. Closing the `CatalogPresentation`-as-code loophole also surfaced two
genuinely unenforced **boolean** flags — `dedicated_outbound_ip` (mail-enterprise) and `dedicated_db`
(managed-woo/shop-peak) — that were previously "read" only by the price list. Labelling them `FAIR_USE` would have
been dishonest (they are not operational facts like "the CDN's PoP count"; they are simply unbuilt). I added them to
`KNOWN_GAPS` instead, using the same ratchet mechanism regardless of PHP type, and documented the deviation in
`PlanPromises`'s docblock and `TASK-0017.md`'s Findings. If the intent was instead to keep these two passing via
`FAIR_USE` or to treat them differently, that is a one-line change in `PlanPromises::KNOWN_GAPS` /
`MetricRegistry::REGISTRY` — flagging rather than deciding unilaterally, since it touches how honestly the guard
represents these two promises.
