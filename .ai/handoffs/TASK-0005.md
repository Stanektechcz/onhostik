**Outcome.** A customer acting on their own service can no longer reach another customer's shell user, database user
or mailbox on the same ISPConfig instance. The panel is never asked about an id the service does not own.

**Files.**

- `providers/IspConfig/IspConfigWebProvider.php` — new private `ownRow()`; `setShellKey`, `setDbUserPassword` and
  `deleteDbUser` resolve the id against the site's own listing first (and `assertWebDomain` like their siblings).
- `domains/Provisioning/Workflows/ServiceActionWorkflow.php` — before the action `match`, `mailbox.update`,
  `mailbox.delete` and `alias.delete` resolve `remote_id` against the service's own mailboxes/aliases across every
  mail domain it holds (`MailDomains::across`), and refuse with a non-retryable step failure.
- `tests/Contract/IspConfigOwnershipTest.php`, `tests/Feature/Provisioning/MailboxOwnershipTest.php` — new.
- `docs/runbooks/production-readiness-audit.md` (row 92), `docs/runbooks/security-boundaries.md` (§19),
  `docs/context/CURRENT_STATE.md`.

**Contracts.** None changed. No endpoint, event payload, schema or config key moves. Two refusal shapes are reused as
they already exist: `ProviderException(NOT_FOUND)` in the adapter, `StepResult::fail(..., retryable: false)` in the saga.

**Verification.**

- VERIFIED — failing-first: 3 of 6 contract tests and 2 of 3 mailbox tests failed on the unfixed code, for the right
  reason (the stranger's id was accepted and the panel was called).
- VERIFIED — after the fix: `tests/Contract/IspConfigOwnershipTest.php` + `tests/Feature/Provisioning/MailboxOwnershipTest.php`
  9/9 green; full suite in this worktree **904 tests / 14 808 assertions green**; Larastan 0 errors; Pint clean.
- VERIFIED — aaPanel read method by method: every id-taking method there resolves against its own listing, and the
  two it cannot do refuse outright. No change needed.
- ASSUMED — that ISPConfig's remote API does not itself enforce `client_id` ownership on `primary_id`. The adapter's
  own comments and its sibling guards say so, and it is the conservative assumption; not reproduced against a live panel.
- NOT TESTED — nothing was run against a live ISPConfig instance; the local instances point at live panels and write
  flows are forbidden from here.

**Concerns.**

1. **Whether this was ever exploited cannot be answered from the code.** `provider_calls` of the last 90 days hold the
   `sites_shell_user_update`, `sites_database_user_update`, `sites_database_user_delete`, `mail_user_update` and
   `mail_user_delete` calls; an operator can compare each `primary_id` with the service that asked. Worth doing before
   go-live.
2. The same audit confirmed twelve further findings that are **not** fixed here — including one ISPConfig client per
   organization whose limits are written from a single service's entitlements (a second hosting cannot be provisioned,
   and a resize lowers the limits for every other site of that organization), and carried sites that are purged thirty
   days after a cancellation the customer undid. Each needs its own task; the first two are P0-looking.
3. The verification pass of that audit refuted **none** of fourteen candidate findings, which is a sign the skeptics
   were too agreeable — treat the remaining twelve as unverified until read in the code, as this one was.
